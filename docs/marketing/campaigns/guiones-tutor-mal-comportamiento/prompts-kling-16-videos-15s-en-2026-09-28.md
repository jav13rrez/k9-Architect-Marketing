# K9 Architect — 16 Kling prompts, English version, ready to paste (15 s)

**Second version of** `prompts-kling-16-videos-15s-2026-09-28.md`. Same tool (Kling 可灵), same pipeline (`cinematic-director` Steps 8–10: keyframe-anchored S1 for shots 1 and 3, first/last-frame pair S3 for shot 2), same identity strings, same staged actions and negatives — written in English instead of Chinese so it's directly readable and editable. Kling supports English (`yes` on text-to-video and image-to-video), just not as its native-tuned language; camera and lighting terms stay stable in English regardless per the skill's own lexicon.

Both files are kept: the Chinese one for anyone who wants the marginal quality edge Kling's own docs claim for native-language prompts, this one for actual day-to-day use.

## How it's built (same as the Chinese version)

- **Shape:** S1 (image-to-video from one keyframe) for shots 1 and 3 of every video; **S3 (first + last frame pair)** for shot 2, the highest-risk shot of each scene — the skill's own adapter names this as the default reason to use a pair.
- **Chain of 3 clips ≈5 s = 15 s total**, hard cut in editing. Brand end card added in post, never generated.
- **Negative field is real on Kling** — everything excluded goes in Kling's negative-prompt field, never as a negation inside the positive prompt (a negation inside the positive text can summon the thing it names).
- **Identity strings** for every dog and tutor follow the `continuity-bible.md` recipe: 30–50 words, fixed physical facts + exactly one checkable landmark + one wardrobe/collar anchor. Paste them **verbatim**, never paraphrased, into every prompt for that character.
- **Shared NEG_BASE** for all 16:

```text
NEG_BASE (negative field): face change, costume change, extra fingers, deformed paws, flicker, jitter, morphing, text, watermark, second dog, sudden camera-angle change
```

- **Bite-risk shots:** shot 2 (S3) always ends *before* contact — the last frame is the instant just before, never the contact itself. Same welfare call as the master script, not a quality trade-off.
- **VO and end card:** not generated by Kling. VO is dubbed separately in post; end card is the brand template added in editing.
- **Word budget:** roughly 40–80 words per motion prompt (image-to-video), per the skill's token-economy rule — cut style adjectives first, never the primary action or the end state.

## V01 · Reactivity from frustration

**ID_DOG:** `A medium-large mixed-breed dog, short tan coat, erect triangular ears, natural short tail vibrating at the tip, a dark grey fabric leash clipped to its collar`

**ID_TUTOR:** `A woman in her early 30s, dark hair tied back, oval face, a faint old scar on the back of her right hand, wearing a dark brown raincoat with the collar turned up`

**LOCK:** `A narrow old European city sidewalk, worn grey paving stones, a few parked bicycles in the background`
**LIGHT:** `Overcast diffuse daylight, no directional shadow, cool color temperature`

### Shot 1 (S1) — trigger

```text
Keyframe 1. Wide shot, eye level, camera at 1.5m, 35mm. Medium-large tan mixed-breed dog, erect triangular ears, natural short tail, dark grey fabric leash on its collar; woman in her early 30s, dark hair tied back, dark brown raincoat, collar up, gripping the leash. Both stand on a narrow stone sidewalk; another dog passes calmly 25m away. The dog's body is rigid, tail tip starting to vibrate, ears forward. The woman's wrist has just wound the leash tighter. Overcast diffuse light, no directional shadow. 9:16.
Negative: face change, costume change, extra fingers, deformed paws, text, watermark, second dog in clear close-up
```

```text
Shot 1 motion. Locked camera, no movement at all. The dog's tail tip keeps vibrating fast for about a second, then the front paws lift half a step off the ground. The woman's right hand slowly winds the leash one more half-turn, knuckles paling. End with the dog's front paws just touching back down, body leaning 15 degrees forward, leash pulled taut. Same face, same raincoat, same dog throughout.
Negative: face change, costume change, flicker, jitter, text, watermark
```

### Shot 2 (S3, first + last frame pair) — escalation

```text
Keyframe A (first). Medium shot, eye level, camera at 1.2m, 50mm. Same dog, front paws already off the ground, body leaning 45 degrees forward, leash a taut 45-degree diagonal, mouth slightly open but not barking. Same woman, feet planted half a step back against the pull, both hands gripping the leash. Overcast diffuse light. 9:16.
Negative: face change, costume change, second dog, text, watermark

Keyframe B (last). Same size, same camera height, same lens. All four of the dog's paws are back on the ground, body leaning back, panting, tongue hanging to one side, eyes unfocused. The woman's shoulders have dropped, the leash hangs slack. Same face, same raincoat, same dog.
Negative: face change, costume change, second dog, text, watermark
```

```text
Shot 2 motion (first-to-last bridge). Between the two frames, the dog completes one forward lunge and lands back down; the leash never breaks free; the other dog has already left frame. Most of the motion happens in the second half; the first half is nearly still. Camera locked, no pan or move. Preserve the same face, same raincoat, same dog, same leash throughout.
Negative: face change, costume change, flicker, second dog clearly in frame, text, watermark
```

### Shot 3 (S1) — aftermath

```text
Keyframe 3. Close-up, eye level, camera at 0.9m, 85mm. Same dog, tongue hanging to one side, panting, eyes unfocused, the sidewalk soft and out of focus behind it. 9:16.
Negative: face change, extra fingers, text, watermark

Shot 3 motion. Locked camera. The dog's chest rises and falls fast at first, then gradually slows; panting eases; the eyes shift from unfocused to slightly more settled. End with the dog closing its mouth, breathing evened out.
Negative: flicker, jitter, text, watermark
```

**Edit:** clip1 (5s) + clip2 (5s, from the pair) + clip3 (5s) = 15s. End card overlaid on the last ~2s of clip3: `K9 ARCHITECT / Un paso cada vez. / Probar con mi perro / Aplicación web · 7 días sin tarjeta`.
